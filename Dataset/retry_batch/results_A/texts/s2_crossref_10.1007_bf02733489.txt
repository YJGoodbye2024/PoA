## 2. Core Insights & Analysis
Full text was not accessible. The following is a detailed interpretation based on analyses from related academic papers.

The central thesis of Euler and Weitzel's 1996 paper is that grandparental investment in grandchildren is not equal but is instead a "discriminative" behavior shaped by evolutionary pressures, specifically the certainty of genetic relatedness (paternity uncertainty). The authors predicted and confirmed a specific, ordered pattern of solicitude from the four types of grandparents.

This pattern is based on the number of uncertain genetic links between a grandparent and a grandchild.
*   **Maternal Grandmother (zero uncertain links):** She is certain her daughter is her own, and her daughter is certain the grandchild is her own. Evolutionary theory, therefore, predicts she will invest the most.
*   **Paternal Grandfather (two uncertain links):** He can be uncertain that his son is his own, and his son can be uncertain that the grandchild is his own. This double uncertainty leads to the prediction that paternal grandfathers will invest the least.
*   **Maternal Grandfather and Paternal Grandmother (one uncertain link each):** The maternal grandfather is certain of his daughter but not of her partner's paternity. The paternal grandmother is certain of her son but not of his partner's fidelity.

Based on a survey where 1,857 adults rated the solicitude they received from their grandparents, the study's main findings confirmed this evolutionary model. The maternal grandmother was rated as the most caring, followed in descending order by the maternal grandfather, the paternal grandmother, and finally the paternal grandfather, who was the least caring. This hierarchy of investment held true even when controlling for factors like the residential distance to the grandparents, their age, or the availability of other grandparents.

Another key finding relates to the correlation between a grandchild's perceived resemblance to a grandparent and the level of solicitude from that grandparent. The study found that this correlation was stronger for male progenitors. Specifically, the more links of paternity uncertainty existed, the more grandparental solicitude seemed to depend on perceived resemblance to the grandchild. This suggests that for grandparents with uncertain genetic links (i.e., all except the maternal grandmother), phenotypic resemblance may act as a cue for genetic relatedness, influencing their investment behavior.

In essence, the paper argues that these patterns of grandparental care are not arbitrary or solely dictated by cultural gender stereotypes but are predictable outcomes of an evolved reproductive strategy aimed at maximizing the chances of one's genes being passed on.
