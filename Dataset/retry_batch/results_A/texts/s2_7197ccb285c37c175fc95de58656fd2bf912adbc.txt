#### **Part 1: Predictions of Social Functioning from Infancy to Middle Childhood**

The first section of the paper summarizes findings from the Uppsala Longitudinal Study (ULS), tracking 96 individuals from infancy to age 9. It investigates how different factors predict later social functioning. The analysis showed that temperament was a relevant predictor for most social outcomes. The role of attachment security was most clearly demonstrated in its connection to social competence and personality development. Additionally, social factors, such as non-parental daycare, were found to have both direct and moderating positive effects on development.

#### **Part 2: Follow-up at Young Adulthood (Age 21)**

The second part of the paper discusses the follow-up with 85 of the original participants at age 21. This section focuses on the developmental origins of social anxiety symptoms.

*   **Shyness and Social Anxiety:** The study confirmed a specific predictive relationship between shyness/inhibition in early life and symptoms of social anxiety in young adulthood. This relationship was found to be specific to social anxiety; the link to depression symptoms did not hold when controlled for social anxiety.
*   **Multi-faceted Models:** The authors emphasize the need for complex models to understand developmental paths. Their broad analysis showed that other temperament factors, beyond shyness, as well as interactive effects, are also relevant in predicting social anxiety.
