## 1. Official Abstract
The purpose of this study was to examine, comparatively, the psychological characteristics of withdrawn, aggressive, and average/normal fifth grade children (M age = 10.6 years). From an initial sample of 224 children, an extreme groups targeting procedure based on peer assessments identified 29 aggressive, 31 withdrawn, and 118 average children. Both the aggressive and withdrawn children were found to be less popular among peers than the average group; however, the aggressive group was the most actively disliked. Teachers rated aggressive children as the most hostile (especially aggressive boys), and as having more learning problems and less tolerance for frustration than the average group. The withdrawn group was rated by teachers as more shy-anxious and less competently assertive, and by peers as less likely to play sociable-leadership roles, than the comparison groups. Withdrawn children also judged themselves as less physically competent than average and aggressive children. Finally, withdrawn boys, but not girls, reported themselves as being more lonely and less socially skilled than their average and aggressive counterparts.

## 2. Core Insights & Analysis
The full text of the paper was accessible for analysis. Below is a synopsis organized by the paper's original sections.

### Introduction

The introduction establishes the context for the study by contrasting the well-documented developmental risks of childhood aggression with the less understood implications of social withdrawal. The authors note that while aggression is a "salient and stable construct" linked to later issues like delinquency and academic problems, the study of social withdrawal has lacked a clear conceptual focus. They differentiate passive withdrawal from immaturity or simple low social interaction, defining it as a consistent display of solitary behavior in the presence of peers. The paper posits that both aggressive and withdrawn children may face peer rejection but for different reasons. It was hypothesized that aggressive children are actively disliked due to their disruptive behaviors, whereas withdrawn children are more likely to be neglected because they are "noninteractive and seemingly uninteresting." The study aimed to create a clearer, comparative portrait of the socioemotional characteristics of extremely withdrawn and extremely aggressive children in later childhood.

### Method

*   **Participants:** The study began with an initial sample of 224 fifth-grade children (110 boys, 114 girls) with a mean age of 10.6 years from three public schools in Waterloo, Ontario.
*   **Procedure:** Children were categorized into three groups—aggressive, withdrawn, and average—based on peer assessments. Children rated each classmate on a 5-point scale for items like "This person likes to start fights" (aggression) and "This person is shy" (withdrawal). This process identified 31 withdrawn children, 29 aggressive children, and 118 average children.
*   **Measures:** A multi-method approach was used to gather data:
    *   **Peer Assessments:** Besides the initial grouping, peer ratings were used to measure sociability-leadership and popularity (how much they were liked or disliked).
    *   **Self-Reports:** Children completed questionnaires to assess their loneliness and self-perceptions of competence in scholastic, social, athletic, and other domains.
    *   **Teacher Ratings:** Teachers completed the Teacher's Rating Scale of Child's Actual Behavior, which assesses factors like hostility, shyness/anxiety, learning problems, and frustration tolerance.

### Results

The study's findings revealed distinct profiles for each group:

*   **Peer Acceptance:** Both aggressive and withdrawn children were significantly less popular than their average peers. However, the aggressive group was the most actively disliked by classmates.
*   **Behavioral Characteristics:**
    *   **Aggressive Children:** Teachers rated them as having significantly more learning problems, lower frustration tolerance, and higher levels of hostility than both withdrawn and average children. Peers also rated them lowest on sociability-leadership.
    *   **Withdrawn Children:** Teachers rated this group as more shy-anxious and less assertively competent than the other groups. Peers perceived them as less likely to take on sociable-leadership roles compared to average children.
*   **Self-Perceptions:**
    *   Withdrawn children rated their own physical competence lower than both aggressive and average children did.
    *   A significant gender difference emerged in self-reports: withdrawn boys reported feeling lonelier and less socially competent than average and aggressive boys. This distinction was not found among girls, where withdrawn girls did not report significantly different levels of loneliness compared to their female peers.

### Discussion

The authors conclude that by the later years of elementary school, both aggression and withdrawal are associated with significant socioemotional difficulties, but the nature of these difficulties differs. Aggressive children face problems that are primarily "externalizing," characterized by active peer rejection and teacher-perceived hostility and learning issues.

In contrast, withdrawn children's difficulties are more "internalizing." They are seen by teachers as anxious and non-assertive, and they perceive themselves as less physically capable. The finding that withdrawn boys, in particular, report heightened loneliness and a lower sense of social skill suggests they may be at greater risk for internalizing problems than withdrawn girls. The authors speculate this may be because the trait of shyness is less socially acceptable for boys in North American culture.

The study underscores that both extreme aggression and extreme withdrawal represent distinct forms of maladjustment. While aggressive children provoke overt negative reactions from their social environment, withdrawn children suffer from a lack of social engagement and negative self-perceptions that may lead to later issues like depression and anxiety.
