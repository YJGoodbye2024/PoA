## 2. Core Insights & Analysis
Full text was accessible. The following is a synopsis of the paper, organized by its original sections.

### I. Introduction
Strotz introduces the central problem of the paper: will an individual, having chosen an optimal consumption plan for the future, actually follow that plan when the time comes to act? He posits that the plan chosen at one moment is often not the plan that will be followed later, leading to "inconsistent" behavior.

This inconsistency can lead to what Strotz calls "spendthriftiness" if the individual does not recognize their own tendency to deviate from their plans. However, if a rational individual *does* recognize this inconsistency, they have two strategies:
1.  **Precommitment:** The individual might take steps to lock themselves into their current plan, deliberately removing future choices to prevent their future self from making a different decision.
2.  **Consistent Planning:** The individual may alter their initial plan to one that they know their future self will willingly follow, even if it's not the "ideal" plan from the present's perspective. This leads to "thrifty" behavior.

Strotz's analysis aims to give formal meaning to these concepts within the theory of utility maximization.

### II. The Utility of the Consumption Plan
This section formalizes the problem. An individual chooses a consumption plan, represented by a function C(t), over a period of time to maximize a utility functional, U. This functional depends on the entire consumption path from the present (time τ) to a future time T.

The utility functional is expressed as an integral of future utilities, discounted back to the present moment:
*U(τ) = ∫<sub>τ</sub><sup>T</sup> λ(t-τ) * u[C(t), t] dt*

Here, *u[C(t), t]* is the "instantaneous utility" of consumption at time *t*, and *λ(t-τ)* is the discount function, which weighs future utility based on its distance from the present moment, τ. The core of the problem lies in the nature of this discount function.

### III. The Possibility of Inconsistency
Strotz demonstrates that inconsistency arises unless the discount function, λ(t), takes a specific, exponential form (λ(t) = k<sup>t</sup>).

He explains that at any given moment, an individual plans to allocate consumption such that the marginal utility of consumption, adjusted for the discount factor, is equal at all future points in time. The problem arises when the individual re-evaluates their plan at a later date. The original optimal plan remains optimal *only if* the relative weighting of utility between any two future dates (say, 2030 and 2031) remains the same whether viewed from 2025 or 2026.

This condition of "dynamic consistency" holds only if the discount function is exponential. For any other form of discount function, a plan that looks optimal today will not look optimal tomorrow, leading the individual to revise it. Strotz refers to this as the individual being in an "intertemporal tussle with himself."

### IV. The Strategy of Consistent Planning
If an individual recognizes their own future inconsistency, they may adopt a strategy of choosing a plan that they know they will not be tempted to abandon. This means selecting a consumption path that will be optimal at every future point in time. The individual treats their own future decisions as a constraint on their present choices. This often results in a higher rate of saving (being more "thrifty") in the present than what would have been planned by a "myopic" or naive individual who assumes they will follow their initial, ideal plan.

### V. The Strategy of Precommitment
The alternative strategy for a rational individual who foresees their inconsistency is precommitment. Strotz defines this as taking actions in the present to "preclude future options" so that their future behavior is forced to align with their present desires. An example would be joining a retirement savings plan that penalizes early withdrawals. By doing so, the individual binds their future self to the current long-term savings goal, preventing a future, more myopic self from spending the money.
