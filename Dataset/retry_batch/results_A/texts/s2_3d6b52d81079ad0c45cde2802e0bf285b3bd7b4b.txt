## 1. Official Abstract
"The current article describes the psychometric qualities of the Creative Experiences Questionnaire (CEQ), a brief 25-item self-report measure of fantasy proneness. Findings indicate that the CEQ demonstrates adequate test-retest stability and internal consistency. CEQ scores appear not to be related to social despotism. The CEQ was found to be strongly correlated with a concurrent measure of fantasy proneness. Furthermore, there are substantial correlations between the CEQ and standard measures of absorption, schizotypy, and dissociation. Bearing in mind that these constructs are thought to be intimately linked to fantasy proneness, this pattern of correlations supports the validity of the CEQ. The CEQ might be fruitfully used as a brief research scale in several domains (e.g. studies on pseudomemories)."

## 2. Core Insights & Analysis
The full text of the paper was accessible for analysis.

### Introduction
The authors begin by highlighting the relevance of fantasy proneness in various psychological phenomena, such as the creation of pseudomemories, paranormal experiences, and its overlap with traits like absorption, dissociativity, and schizotypy. They note that most research at the time relied on versions of the Inventory of Childhood Memories and Imaginings (ICMI), but found it difficult to locate consistent versions and their corresponding psychometric data. The ICMI was also considered a lengthy scale. Motivated by these challenges, the researchers developed the Creative Experiences Questionnaire (CEQ) as a "brief research instrument for measuring fantasy proneness."

### Method
The paper details the development and validation of the 25-item CEQ, which uses a simple "yes/no" response format. The validation process involved several studies:
*   **Study 1 (Test-Retest Reliability):** A group of 17 undergraduate students completed the CEQ twice with a six-week interval.
*   **Study 2 (Internal Consistency and Group Differences):** The CEQ was administered to a large sample of 405 undergraduate students. It was also given to specific groups expected to have high fantasy proneness: 20 individuals reporting paranormal experiences, 15 amateur actors, and 15 members of a fantasy gaming club.
*   **Subsequent Studies (Concurrent Validity):** The researchers summarize results from separate studies where they compared CEQ scores with other established psychological scales. This included correlating the CEQ with the ICMI, the Tellegen Absorption Scale (TAS), the Claridge schizotypal personality scale (STA), and the Dissociative Experiences Scale (DES).

### Results
The psychometric properties of the CEQ were found to be robust:
*   **Reliability:** The test-retest reliability over six weeks was very high (r = 0.96). The internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha) was found to be adequate at 0.76. The scores were not found to be correlated with social desirability.
*   **Group Differences:** As hypothesized, the groups of people with paranormal experiences, amateur actors, and fantasy gamers all scored significantly higher on the CEQ than the general undergraduate student sample, supporting the scale's predictive validity.
*   **Concurrent Validity:** The CEQ showed strong correlations with other relevant measures. It had a high correlation of 0.77 with the ICMI, confirming it measures a similar construct. Furthermore, significant correlations were found with absorption (r = 0.61), schizotypy (r = 0.61), and dissociation (r = 0.48), which aligns with the theoretical understanding of fantasy proneness being linked to these traits.

### Discussion
The authors conclude that the CEQ is a "psychometrically sound research instrument." They summarize its strengths: it has good reliability (test-retest and internal consistency), predictive validity (differentiating between high and low fantasy-prone groups), and concurrent validity (correlating well with other measures of fantasy proneness and related concepts). The paper suggests the CEQ is a useful and efficient tool for research in areas like the study of paranormal beliefs and the experimental analysis of false memories ("pseudomemories").
