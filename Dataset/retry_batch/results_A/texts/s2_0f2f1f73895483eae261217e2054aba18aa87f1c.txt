## 1. Official 'bstract
> "traditionally, researchers have associated creativity with defocused attention. Recent experimental evidence contradicts this notion by demonstrating that in creative people, defocused attention is a variable state rather than a stable trait. Specifically, creative people are better at adjusting their focus of attention as a function of task demands. When the task is ill defined and ambiguity is high, attention is defocused, resulting in slower processing on the task. In contrast, when the task is well defined and ambiguity is low, attention is focused, resulting in faster processing on the task. This flexibility can confer distinct advantages to creative people in the course of problem solving as changes in the structure of the problem necessitate corresponding adjustments in solution strategy."

(Source: ResearchGate, American Psychological Association)

## 2. Core Insights & Analysis
Full text was not accessible. The following is a detailed interpretation based on analysis from citing academic works and secondary sources.

The core argument of Vartanian's 2009 paper, "Variable attention facilitates creative problem solving," is a significant departure from the traditional view that creativity is simply a product of defocused attention. Instead, the paper posits that the hallmark of a creative individual is the ability to flexibly modulate their attentional focus according to the specific demands of a task. This concept is often referred to as "variable attention" or "flexible cognitive control."

The paper argues that creative problem-solving is not a monolithic process but one that involves different phases requiring different cognitive approaches. The key insights are:

*   **Defocused Attention for Ill-Defined Problems:** When a problem is ambiguous and its structure is not clear, creative individuals tend to adopt a defocused attentional state. This "leaky" or broad attention allows them to take in a wider range of information, including seemingly irrelevant details, which can serve as the building blocks for novel solutions. This phase is associated with slower, more exploratory processing.
*   **Focused Attention for Well-Defined Problems:** Conversely, when a task is clearly defined and has low ambiguity, creative people can narrow their attentional focus. This focused state enables faster, more efficient processing, allowing for the critical evaluation and refinement of ideas.
*   **Flexibility as the Key Advantage:** The crucial advantage for creative individuals is not a permanent state of defocused attention, but the capacity to switch between these focused and defocused modes as the problem-solving process evolves. Vartanian suggests that this strategic flexibility allows for both the playful exploration of ideas and the extraordinary focus needed to execute them.

In essence, Vartanian's work reframes the relationship between attention and creativity. It moves beyond a static trait (defocused attention) to a dynamic, variable state. This flexibility allows creative people to adapt their cognitive strategy, broadening their attention to generate possibilities when a problem is vague and narrowing it to select and implement a solution when the path becomes clearer. This model has been influential in subsequent neuroscience and psychology research, which further explores how the brain supports the flexible cognitive control necessary for creative thought.
