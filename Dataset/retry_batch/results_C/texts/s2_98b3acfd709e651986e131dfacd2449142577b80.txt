## 1. Official Abstract
Openness to experience is the broadest personality domain of the Big Five, including a mix of traits relating to intellectual curiosity, intellectual interests, perceived intelligence, imagination, creativity, artistic and aesthetic interests, emotional and fantasy richness, and unconventionality. Likewise, creative achievement is a broad construct, comprising creativity across the arts and sciences. The aim of this study was to clarify the relationship between openness to experience and creative achievement. A new theoretical framework was proposed, consisting of a four-factor model of openness to experience. The four factors—Intellectual Engagement, Affective Engagement, Aesthetic Engagement, and Explicit Cognitive Ability—were derived from a factor analysis of a wide range of traits previously associated with openness to experience. The four factors showed different relations to creative achievement in the arts and sciences. Intellectual Engagement and Explicit Cognitive Ability were positively related to creative achievement in the sciences, whereas Affective Engagement and Aesthetic Engagement were positively related to creative achievement in the arts. These results support a more nuanced view of the connection between openness to experience and creativity, and have important implications for the identification and nurturing of creative potential across all domains.

### Detailed Interpretation

The paper "Opening up Openness to Experience" by Scott Barry Kaufman introduces a more detailed model for understanding the personality trait of Openness to Experience, which is one of the Big Five personality domains. Traditionally, Openness is seen as a broad trait encompassing everything from intellectual curiosity to artistic interests. Kaufman argues for a more nuanced view, proposing that this single trait can be broken down into more specific components, which in turn relate differently to creative achievement in various fields.

**The Four-Factor Model**

The core of the paper is the proposal and validation of a four-factor model of Openness to Experience. Through factor analysis of a wide array of traits associated with Openness, Kaufman identified four distinct, though related, factors:

1.  **Intellectual Engagement:** This factor relates to intellectual curiosity and the enjoyment of abstract and complex ideas.
2.  **Affective Engagement:** This involves emotional richness, empathy, and a deep appreciation for feelings.
3.  **Aesthetic Engagement:** This is characterized by a strong interest in art, beauty, and aesthetics.
4.  **Explicit Cognitive Ability:** This factor is linked to perceived intelligence and actual performance on cognitive tests.

**Relationship to Creative Achievement**

The study's main finding is that these four factors have different predictive relationships with creative success in the arts versus the sciences. The research used the Creative Achievement Questionnaire (CAQ) to measure self-reported accomplishments in both domains.

*   **Creative Achievement in the Sciences:** This was positively predicted by **Intellectual Engagement** and **Explicit Cognitive Ability**. This suggests that scientific creativity is more closely tied to intellectual curiosity and cognitive horsepower.
*   **Creative Achievement in the Arts:** This was positively predicted by **Affective Engagement** and **Aesthetic Engagement**. This indicates that artistic creativity is more strongly linked to emotional depth and an appreciation for beauty. Interestingly, one analysis noted that Affective Engagement was *negatively* related to scientific achievement.

This differential prediction is a key insight of the paper. It refines the broader finding that "Openness" predicts creativity by showing *which aspects* of Openness are relevant to *which type* of creativity. This work builds on and clarifies the "Openness/Intellect" split proposed by other researchers, which distinguishes between the cognitive (Intellect) and perceptual/experiential (Openness) sides of the trait. Kaufman's model provides a more granular framework by further dividing these aspects.

In essence, the paper argues against treating Openness to Experience as a monolithic trait when studying creativity. By "opening it up," researchers and educators can better understand, identify, and foster the specific dispositions that lead to creative success in both the arts and the sciences.
