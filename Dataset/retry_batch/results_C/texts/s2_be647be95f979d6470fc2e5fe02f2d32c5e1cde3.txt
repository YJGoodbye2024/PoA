## 1. Official Abstract
"This chapter examines the feeling of being grateful. It suggests feeling grateful is similar to other positive emotions that help build a person's enduring personal resources and broaden an individual's thinking. It describes various ways by which gratitude can transform individuals, organizations, and communities in positive and sustaining ways. It discusses the specific benefits of gratitude including personal and social development, community strength and individual health and well-being."

## 2. Core Insights & Analysis
Full text was not accessible. The following is a detailed interpretation based on secondary sources that cite and analyze the work.

The 2004 chapter, "Gratitude, Like Other Positive Emotions, Broadens and Builds," is a specific application of Dr. Barbara Fredrickson's comprehensive **Broaden-and-Build Theory of Positive Emotions**. The core argument is that gratitude, akin to emotions like joy, interest, and love, contributes to human flourishing by expanding cognitive and behavioral repertoires, which in turn builds lasting personal resources.

**The "Broaden" Effect of Gratitude:**
Fredrickson's theory posits that while negative emotions narrow our focus to handle immediate threats (e.g., fear prompts an urge to escape), positive emotions have a different, complementary purpose. They broaden our "momentary thought-action repertoires," making us more open to new ideas, more creative, and more flexible in our thinking. When experiencing gratitude, an individual is not focused on a narrow, survival-oriented action but is instead more likely to think expansively. This broadened mindset allows for greater awareness and the ability to see a bigger picture. For example, gratitude can shift the emotional climate in relationships from criticism to connection and foster prosocial behaviors that support community well-being.

**The "Build" Effect of Gratitude:**
The "broaden" effect is temporary, but its consequences are lasting. By encouraging broadened thinking and action, gratitude helps to "build" durable personal resources over time. These resources are not just psychological but also social, intellectual, and physical.
*   **Social Resources:** Shared experiences of gratitude can build stronger social bonds and attachments. By reinforcing prosocial actions, gratitude helps create and sustain mutually beneficial relationships within a community.
*   **Psychological Resources:** The accumulation of positive emotional experiences, including gratitude, builds resilience. It provides an "undoing effect" against the lingering physiological impacts of negative emotions and helps individuals cope more effectively with adversity.
*   **Intellectual and Physical Resources:** By promoting exploration and new ways of thinking, positive emotions can lead to the development of new skills and knowledge. This process contributes to overall health and well-being.

Ultimately, Fredrickson frames gratitude within an "upward spiral." Experiencing gratitude broadens the mind, which builds resources. These resources, in turn, make it more likely for a person to experience future positive emotions, leading to sustained personal growth and optimal functioning.
