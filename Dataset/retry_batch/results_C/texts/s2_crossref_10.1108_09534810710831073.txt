### Detailed Interpretation

**The Concept of Savoring**
The central thesis of Bryant and Veroff's model is the concept of "savoring," defined as the capacity to attend to, appreciate, and enhance the positive experiences in one's life. It is the regulation of positive feelings through specific cognitive or behavioral responses to positive events. The authors argue that a savoring experience can be a reaction to a direct sensory input or can be derived from the cognitive associations an experience brings to mind. For instance, the beauty of a painting is savored not just for its visual qualities, but also for the associated feelings and knowledge it evokes. The experience of savoring is enhanced when the positive feelings are shared or reciprocated. Research building on this model has confirmed that savoring is associated with increased positive emotions and is a key mechanism through which people derive happiness from positive events.

**Savoring Across Time**
A key element of the model is the relationship between savoring and one's perception of time. Bryant and Veroff use their "Savoring Beliefs Inventory (SBI)" to explore this connection. The model identifies three temporal orientations for savoring:
*   **Past-focused (Reminiscing):** Reflecting on positive memories. The authors caution that while this can enrich the present, excessive reminiscing to escape current negative feelings is considered maladaptive.
*   **Present-focused (Savoring the Moment):** Being mindful of and elaborating on a current positive experience as it happens.
*   **Future-focused (Anticipating):** Enjoying the expectation of an upcoming positive event. Having dreams about the future is presented as a form of savoring, though the authors note that too much anticipation can reduce the joy of surprise.

**Savoring Across the Lifespan**
The model also explores how the ability to savor develops and changes throughout a person's life. Bryant and Veroff propose that true savoring can only begin in childhood once an individual is capable of being mindful of the present moment and can articulate their experience. They suggest that in older adults, savoring becomes a more conscious and active process, as they are more inclined to reflect on both positive and negative life experiences.

**Applications and Legacy**
Since the publication of "Savoring: A New Model of Positive Experience," the concept has become a core topic in positive psychology. The Savoring Beliefs Inventory (SBI) has been translated into numerous languages and is widely used in cross-cultural research. Researchers in organizational behavior have applied the model to study topics such as work-family conflict, job performance, and organizational commitment, and have proposed strategies for organizations to enhance savoring among employees.
