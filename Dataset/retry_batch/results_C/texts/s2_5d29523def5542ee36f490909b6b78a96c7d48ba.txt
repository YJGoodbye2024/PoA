Full text was not accessible. The following is a detailed interpretation based on analysis from citing papers and academic databases.

The 2008 paper, "Toward a Theory of Entertainment Persuasion," by Emily Moyer-Gusé is a foundational theoretical work in the field of communication that seeks to explain how and why entertainment-education (E-E) is effective as a tool for persuasion. The paper addresses the observation that embedding health or prosocial messages within an entertainment narrative can be more influential than direct, overt persuasive arguments. Its primary contribution is the introduction and formalization of the **Entertainment Overcoming Resistance Model (EORM)**.

The analysis can be broken down into the paper's key objectives and theoretical propositions as interpreted from secondary sources:

**1. Clarification of "Involvement" Concepts:**
A central goal of the paper is to bring conceptual clarity to the various ways audiences engage with narratives. Moyer-Gusé distinguishes between several related but distinct concepts that are often used interchangeably in communication literature:
*   **Transportation:** The feeling of being absorbed or immersed into the world of the story, losing awareness of one's immediate surroundings.
*   **Identification:** Taking on the perspective of a character, seeing events through their eyes, and feeling their emotions.
*   **Parasocial Interaction:** The feeling of having a one-sided friendship or relationship with a media figure or character.
*   **Similarity:** The degree to which a viewer perceives themselves as being similar to a character.

The paper argues that these different forms of involvement are crucial mechanisms that drive the persuasive outcomes of entertainment-education.

**2. Synthesis of Existing Theories:**
Moyer-Gusé reviews and integrates previous theoretical frameworks that have been used to explain narrative persuasion. The most prominent of these is the **Extended Elaboration Likelihood Model (E-ELM)**, which posits that the narrative qualities of entertainment can reduce a viewer's tendency to counter-argue the embedded message. The paper also builds upon Bandura's Social Cognitive Theory, which explains learning through observation of models in media.

**3. The Entertainment Overcoming Resistance Model (EORM):**
Building on these prior theories, Moyer-Gusé proposes the EORM as a more comprehensive framework. The model's central argument is that the power of entertainment-education lies in its ability to reduce or overcome various forms of audience resistance to persuasion. The key mechanisms are:
*   **Reducing Counterarguing:** By engaging viewers in a compelling story (transportation) and fostering a connection with characters (identification), the narrative consumes cognitive resources that would otherwise be used to critique and generate counterarguments against the persuasive message.
*   **Overcoming Reactance:** Because the persuasive message is embedded within a story that is perceived primarily as entertainment, viewers are less likely to feel their freedom of choice is being threatened. This lowers psychological reactance, which is a common response to overt persuasive attempts.
*   **Minimizing Perceived Invulnerability:** By having viewers identify with a vulnerable character who suffers the consequences of risky behavior, E-E narratives can increase the audience's own sense of vulnerability to the threat, making the health message more salient.

In essence, the EORM posits that involvement with the narrative and its characters is the key that unlocks persuasion by disarming the audience's natural defenses against being influenced. This theoretical paper concludes by offering a series of propositions to guide future empirical research to test and refine the model.
