## 1. Official Abstract
"One hundred and eleven individuals completed the NEO Five Factor Inventory along with three measures of subjective well-being, the Oxford Happiness Inventory, the Depression–Happiness Scale, and the Satisfaction With Life Scale. Regression analysis showed that although Extraversion and Neuroticism were the strongest predictors of scores on the three measures, Conscientiousness also accounted for unique variance in scores on the Depression–Happiness Scale and the Satisfaction With Life Scale. It is concluded that Conscientiousness is a more important correlate of subjective well-being than previously supposed."

## 2. Core Insights & Analysis
The full text of the paper was accessible and has been analyzed to provide the following synopsis, structured according to the paper's original sections.

### Introduction

The authors begin by noting that while personality is a known correlate of subjective well-being (SWB), research has primarily focused on the traits of Extraversion and Neuroticism. They cite a meta-analysis by DeNeve and Cooper (1998) which confirmed that Extraversion and Neuroticism are the most significant "Big 5" predictors of happiness. However, the same meta-analysis suggested that Conscientiousness is one of the strongest predictors of life satisfaction. The paper states that further investigation is needed to confirm these findings. Therefore, the stated aim of the research is "to extend the investigation of personality correlates of happiness to the Big 5 personality dimensions, Extraversion, Neuroticism, Conscientiousness, Agreeableness, and Openness."

### Method

The study involved 129 residents of Warwickshire, UK, and their acquaintances, recruited through various social and leisure groups. Of these, 111 individuals (39 men, 72 women) with an average age of 32.5 years completed all the required measures.

Participants completed four questionnaires:
1.  **NEO Five-Factor Inventory (NEO-FFI):** This measured the "Big 5" personality traits: Neuroticism (N), Extraversion (E), Openness to Experience (O), Agreeableness (A), and Conscientiousness (C).
2.  **Oxford Happiness Inventory (OHI):** A 29-item scale that serves as a global measure of happiness, assessing both cognitive and affective aspects.
3.  **Depression–Happiness Scale (DHS):** A 25-item global measure of happiness, similar to the OHI.
4.  **Satisfaction With Life Scale (SWLS):** This 5-item scale assesses the cognitive-evaluative component of happiness.

Higher scores on the OHI, DHS, and SWLS indicate greater subjective well-being.

### Results

The analysis first confirmed that the three measures of SWB were highly related to each other. The primary analysis involved correlations and regression analyses to examine the relationship between the Big 5 personality traits and the three SWB measures, after controlling for age and sex.

Key findings from the correlational analysis were:
*   Higher scores on all three SWB measures were associated with higher Extraversion, lower Neuroticism, and higher Conscientiousness.
*   Agreeableness was positively correlated with scores on the OHI and DHS, but not the SWLS.
*   Openness to Experience showed no significant correlation with any of the three SWB measures.

A series of hierarchical regression analyses were then performed for each SWB measure. Extraversion and Neuroticism were entered in the first step, followed by the remaining three personality traits (Openness, Agreeableness, and Conscientiousness) in the second step.

*   **For the Oxford Happiness Inventory (OHI):** Extraversion and Neuroticism accounted for 54% of the variance. The addition of the other three traits did not significantly increase the explained variance.
*   **For the Depression–Happiness Scale (DHS):** Extraversion and Neuroticism accounted for 60% of the variance. In the second step, Conscientiousness was found to account for an additional 2% of unique variance.
*   **For the Satisfaction With Life Scale (SWLS):** Extraversion and Neuroticism explained 41% of the variance. Conscientiousness was again a significant predictor in the second step, accounting for an additional 4% of unique variance.

### Discussion

The authors conclude that their findings confirm the robust roles of Extraversion and Neuroticism as the primary personality correlates of subjective well-being. However, the study's main contribution is highlighting the independent role of Conscientiousness. The results showed that Conscientiousness explained a unique portion of the variance in two of the three SWB measures (DHS and SWLS), even after the strong effects of Extraversion and Neuroticism were accounted for.

The paper suggests that "conscientious individuals are more likely to function effectively in society and to achieve their goals and that this leads to greater happiness." The findings indicate that Conscientiousness may be a more important factor in subjective well-being than previously acknowledged and that its link to life satisfaction, in particular, is noteworthy. The study supports the idea that different components of subjective well-being (global happiness vs. life satisfaction) have slightly different personality correlates.
