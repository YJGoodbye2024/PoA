## 1. Official Abstract
This study examined whether and how McCrae and Costa's Big Five personality dimensions (N, E, O, A, and C) are associated with stress and coping processes, including cognitive appraisals, subjective reactions, use of coping strategies, and task performance. Participants were 97 male and female university undergraduates who completed an abbreviated version of the NEO-PI prior to preparing and presenting a speech to an audience. Immediately after their speeches, participants reported their emotional reactions and the coping strategies used during the task. Two independent coders rated participants' speech task performances. Results indicated that higher levels of N were associated with appraising the task as more threatening, reporting more negative and less positive emotion, and using more emotion-focused and less problem-focused coping. Higher levels of E were associated with appraising the task as more challenging, reporting more positive emotion, and using more problem-focused coping. Higher levels of C were associated with appraising the task as more challenging and better task performance. Finally, higher levels of O were associated with using more humor to cope with the task. A was not significantly associated with any of the stress and coping variables.

The 2002 study by Julie A. Penley and Joe Tomaka investigated the relationships between the Big Five personality traits (Neuroticism, Extraversion, Openness, Agreeableness, and Conscientiousness), emotional responses, and coping mechanisms during an acute stress situation. The study's methodology involved 97 undergraduate students who first completed a personality inventory and were then subjected to a stressful task: preparing and delivering a speech. Their emotional reactions and coping strategies were assessed immediately after the task.

**Key Findings from Citing Sources:**

*   **Neuroticism (N):** Consistent with its definition, higher levels of neuroticism were strongly linked to negative stress responses. Individuals high in this trait were more likely to appraise the stressful situation as a threat, report more negative emotions, and rely more on emotion-focused coping strategies rather than problem-focused ones. This aligns with broader research that identifies neuroticism as a risk factor for psychological distress due to tendencies toward emotional instability and negative emotional reactions to stressors.

*   **Extraversion (E):** In contrast to neuroticism, extraversion was associated with more positive and adaptive responses to stress. Highly extraverted individuals tended to appraise the speech task as a challenge rather than a threat. They also reported experiencing more positive emotions (like happiness and pride) and utilized more problem-focused coping strategies.

*   **Conscientiousness (C):** This trait was linked to more effective and goal-oriented behavior under stress. Those with higher conscientiousness levels also appraised the stressor as more of a challenge and, significantly, demonstrated better performance in the speech task. Other research citing this study notes that conscientious individuals are known to use more effective coping strategies, which may explain their better outcomes when dealing with stress.

*   **Openness to Experience (O):** Individuals high in openness were found to perceive stressful situations more as challenges than threats. The study specifically found that these individuals were more likely to use humor as a coping mechanism during the stressful task.

*   **Agreeableness (A):** In this particular study, agreeableness was not found to have any significant association with the stress appraisal, emotional response, or coping variables measured.

In summary, the Penley and Tomaka (2002) study provides specific evidence for how personality traits predict how an individual will appraise, emotionally react to, and cope with an acute stressor. Its findings are frequently cited to support the model that neuroticism predisposes individuals to negative stress cycles, while extraversion and conscientiousness are linked to more resilient and adaptive responses.
