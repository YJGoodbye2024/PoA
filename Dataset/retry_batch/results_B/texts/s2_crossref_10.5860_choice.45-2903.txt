The book *Attachment in Adulthood: Structure, Dynamics, and Change* by Mario Mikulincer and Phillip R. Shaver is recognized as a definitive reference work that synthesizes a vast body of empirical research on adult attachment theory. The authors, both leading researchers in the field, provide a comprehensive conceptual model that extends the foundational principles of John Bowlby's attachment theory, which originally focused on child development, to understand social functioning across the entire human lifespan.

The core of the book is its explanation of how attachment theory can be used to conceptualize and study nearly all aspects of a person's life. It details how individual differences in adult attachment—often categorized into secure and insecure (anxious and avoidant) styles—influence a wide array of psychological and social domains. These include:
*   **Intrapersonal Processes:** The book examines how attachment patterns shape mental representations of self and others, emotion regulation strategies, personal goals, and the ability to cope with stress. Secure attachment is presented as a crucial internal resource that fosters resilience and effective management of negative emotions.
*   **Interpersonal Dynamics:** It explores the manifestation of attachment styles in close relationships, including romantic partnerships, caregiving, and sexuality. The work traces how early bonding experiences create "internal working models" that guide expectations and behaviors in adult relationships.
*   **Clinical Applications:** Mikulincer and Shaver analyze the links between insecure attachment and various forms of psychopathology. They also discuss the application of attachment principles in counseling and psychotherapy, providing a framework for understanding and addressing relational issues in a clinical context.

The book is noted for its systematic organization, tracing the evolution of Bowlby's original constructs and reviewing the various measures used in adult attachment research. It stands as an essential text for students, researchers, and clinicians, offering a thorough summary of the history, core concepts, and extensive research findings within the field of adult attachment. A second edition, published in 2016, further updated these concepts with new research on the genetic, neural, and hormonal substrates of attachment.
