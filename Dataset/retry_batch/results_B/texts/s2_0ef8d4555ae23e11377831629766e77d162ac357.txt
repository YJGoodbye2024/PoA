## 1. Official Abstract
"This meta-analysis aimed to address the association between personality traits and conflict handling styles. ProQuest, PsycINFO, Scopus, and Web of science and also Microsoft Academic, Science.gov, Science open were searched for all published and non-published studies through 17 October 2018. A literature search located 20 eligible studies, and 5337 total participants. The results of this review show that neuroticism and agreeableness are positively related to avoiding style. The positive associations are observed between agreeableness, extroversion, openness to experience, and conscientiousness with compromising style."

## 2. Core Insights & Analysis
Full text was not accessible. The following is a detailed interpretation based on the paper's abstract, dataset description, and its citation in related academic works.

This meta-analysis systematically investigated the relationship between the Big Five personality traits (Neuroticism, Extraversion, Openness to Experience, Agreeableness, and Conscientiousness) and various conflict resolution styles. The research synthesized data from 20 different studies, encompassing a total of 5,337 participants, to draw robust conclusions about these associations.

The core findings are organized by conflict resolution style:

*   **Avoiding Style**: This style, characterized by withdrawing from or ignoring conflict, was found to be positively associated with both Neuroticism and Agreeableness. Individuals high in neuroticism often adopt avoidant strategies, a finding that is frequently cited by subsequent research.
*   **Compromising Style**: A positive correlation was found between the compromising style and four of the five personality traits: Agreeableness, Extraversion, Openness to Experience, and Conscientiousness.
*   **Integrating Style**: This collaborative style was positively correlated with Agreeableness, Extraversion, Openness to Experience, and Conscientiousness. Conversely, a negative relationship was identified between Neuroticism and the integrating style, suggesting that individuals with higher neuroticism are less likely to use this approach.
*   **Dominating Style**: The analysis revealed a positive relationship between Extraversion and a dominating conflict style. In contrast, Agreeableness was found to be negatively related to the dominating style, which aligns with the cooperative and peace-seeking nature of agreeable individuals.
*   **Obliging Style**: A positive link was identified specifically between Agreeableness and the obliging (or accommodating) style.

The study also performed a moderator analysis, which indicated that the context of the study (work vs. academic settings) could alter the relationship between certain traits and styles. For instance, the link between Agreeableness and the compromising style showed different patterns between work and academic environments.

To ensure the validity of their findings, the authors conducted several tests for publication bias, including the trim and fill method and Egger's linear regression test, concluding that the effect of small-study bias was negligible.
