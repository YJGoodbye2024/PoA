G. P. Watkins's 1922 article in *The Quarterly Journal of Economics* is a critical review of Frank H. Knight's seminal book, *Risk, Uncertainty, and Profit*. While ultimately viewing the book as a significant work, Watkins's review is notable for its specific critiques and qualified praise.

**Critique of Core Concepts:**
The central theme of Watkins's analysis is a critique of Knight's famous distinction between "risk" (a quantifiable probability) and "uncertainty" (an unquantifiable probability) and its direct application to the theory of profit. Watkins was critical of several key aspects of this distinction, particularly concerning how Knight used it to explain the existence of business profit. He appears to have questioned the practical rigidity of the distinction and its role as the sole determinant of entrepreneurial returns.

**Assessment of the Book's Structure and Content:**
Watkins also commented on the structure of Knight's book. He suggested that the first two parts, which lay the theoretical groundwork in a world without uncertainty, could be largely skipped by readers already well-versed in economics. This implies that Watkins saw the true novelty of Knight's work as being concentrated in the third part of the book, where the concept of "true uncertainty" is introduced to explain imperfect competition and profit.

**Overall Conclusion:**
Despite these criticisms, Watkins's review was ultimately favorable. He concluded that Frank H. Knight's book was a "notable contribution to economic theory". Therefore, while Watkins's review highlighted what he perceived as weaknesses in the foundational arguments and structure, it also affirmed the book's importance and its innovative contribution to understanding the nature of profit and the role of the entrepreneur.
